Digital Safety Tips

Essential practices to protect yourself from online threats

Core Safety Principles

🔒

Never Share OTPs

One-Time Passwords are for your use only. Legitimate organizations will never ask you to share your OTP with anyone, including customer support.

🔐

Protect Your Passwords

Use unique, strong passwords for each account. Never share passwords via email, chat, or phone. Enable two-factor authentication wherever possible.

🔍

Verify Before You Click

Always check URLs carefully before clicking. Look for HTTPS, verify the domain name, and be suspicious of shortened links from unknown sources.

⏰

Question Urgency

Scammers create false urgency to make you act without thinking. Take time to verify independently, even if the message claims immediate action is required.

URL and Link Safety

🔗

Check the Domain

Look carefully at the domain name. Scammers often use slight misspellings (e.g., "g0ogle.com" instead of "google.com") or different extensions.

🔒

Look for HTTPS

Ensure websites use HTTPS (look for the lock icon). However, remember that HTTPS alone doesn't guarantee a site is legitimate.

📱

Use URL Scanners

Before visiting suspicious links, use a URL scanner to check for known threats and suspicious patterns.

🖱️

Hover Before Clicking

Hover over links to see the actual destination before clicking, especially in emails and messages.

QR Code Safety

📱

Scan Before You Click

Use a QR scanner that shows you the destination URL before you visit it. Never blindly scan QR codes from unknown sources.

🏪

Be Wary of Public QR Codes

QR codes in public places can be tampered with or replaced by scammers. Be especially cautious with payment QR codes.

💳

Verify Payment QR Codes

Before making payments via QR code, verify the recipient and amount independently through official channels.

🔍

Check the Destination

Always verify where a QR code leads before entering any information or making payments.

Message and Email Safety

📧

Verify the Sender

Check the sender's email address carefully. Scammers often use addresses that look similar to legitimate ones.

🚫

Don't Click Suspicious Links

Avoid clicking links in unsolicited messages. Instead, visit the official website directly through your browser.

📝

Watch for Poor Grammar

Many scam messages contain spelling errors, poor grammar, or awkward phrasing. Legitimate organizations typically have professional communications.

🎯

Personalization Red Flags

Be suspicious of generic greetings like "Dear Customer" instead of your name, though some sophisticated scams do personalize.

Password Security

🔑

Use Strong, Unique Passwords

Create complex passwords with a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols. Never reuse passwords across different sites.

📱

Enable Two-Factor Authentication

Turn on 2FA wherever possible. It adds an extra layer of security even if your password is compromised.

🗄️

Use a Password Manager

Consider using a reputable password manager to generate and store complex passwords securely.

🔄

Change Passwords After Breaches

If a service you use suffers a data breach, change your password immediately for that account and any others using the same password.

Online Shopping Safety

🛒

Research the Seller

Before buying from a new online store, research the company, read reviews from independent sources, and check for contact information.

💳

Use Secure Payment Methods

Prefer credit cards over debit cards for online purchases, as they offer better fraud protection. Avoid wire transfers and cryptocurrency for shopping.

💰

Be Skeptical of Deals

If a price seems too good to be true, it probably is. Unrealistic discounts are a common sign of fake stores.

📦

Track Officially

Track deliveries through the official courier's website, not through links in messages or emails.

Social Media Safety

👥

Be Careful What You Share

Limit the personal information you share on social media. Scammers can use this information for targeted attacks.

🔗

Verify Account Requests

Be suspicious of unexpected friend requests or messages, even from people you know, as their accounts may be compromised.

🎁

Watch for Giveaway Scams

Be skeptical of giveaways that require you to share personal information, pay fees, or click suspicious links.

📱

Check Verification Badges

Scammers may fake verification badges. Check the platform's official verification criteria and report suspicious accounts.

Mobile Device Security

📱

Keep Software Updated

Regularly update your operating system and apps to protect against the latest security vulnerabilities.

🔒

Use Screen Locks

Enable strong screen locks (PIN, pattern, or biometrics) to prevent unauthorized access if your device is lost or stolen.

📲

Download from Official Sources

Only download apps from official app stores. Avoid sideloading apps from unknown sources.

🔍

Review App Permissions

Regularly review and limit app permissions. Be suspicious of apps requesting unnecessary access to your data.

What to Do If You've Been Scammed

1

Act Quickly

Time is critical. The sooner you act, the better chance you have of minimizing damage.

2

Secure Your Accounts

Change passwords for affected accounts and enable two-factor authentication.

3

Contact Your Bank

If financial information was compromised, contact your bank immediately to freeze accounts or cards.

4

Report the Incident

Report the scam to relevant authorities and platforms to help prevent others from being victimized.

5

Monitor Your Accounts

Keep a close eye on your financial accounts and credit reports for suspicious activity.
